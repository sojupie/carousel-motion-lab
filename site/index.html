Investigating carousel settling on iOS

Dragging feels smooth. The final part of the release can hold, step forward, or jump. This page isolates the scrolling and animation paths behind that behavior.

The goal is a carousel with crisp, changing card masks and smooth motion through drag, release, and interruption. Vertical page scrolling, keyboard controls, right-to-left layout, and reduced motion must keep working.

What has been observed

Two related failures

Stepping near the snap target

iPhone and iPad traces report whole-CSS-pixel native offsets during settling. Near the endpoint, one-pixel changes can arrive 50–116 ms apart while JavaScript frame callbacks continue. Rebuilding card geometry from that signal produces repeated positions followed by small advances.

Competing motion after release

Touch handoff replaces the visual settle with fractional animation, but native scrolling can continue underneath it. Some captures show a 6–7 px reversal after the custom animation finishes. At the first item, a recording shows a much larger rubber-band displacement.

The cropped clip shows the return from item 2 to item 1. The paired trace reports native overscroll reaching −275 CSS px, even though the first card’s assigned translation remains zero. Smooth style values alone do not establish smooth rendered motion.

Device results and limits

The issue has been reported on iPhone 15, iPhone 13 Pro Max, and iPad Air (5th generation), across iOS Safari, Chrome, Firefox, and Brave tests. It was not observed on a MacBook Air M4. These are user observations, not a controlled browser benchmark.

Native scrolling and Touch handoff felt smoother in initial phone tests. Touch handoff also produced the overscroll failure shown above. Desktop mouse releases use a custom animation clock, while Baseline touch releases use native inertia and snapping; those paths are not a like-for-like platform comparison.

The evidence supports a sampled-offset and motion-ownership problem. It does not establish a universal browser defect, exact compositor timing, or a finished fix.

How the comparison works

One variable at a time

The masked versions use a sticky visual track and update card masks from logical scroll positions. Native scrolling puts fixed-width cards in normal flow. Rounded clock deliberately removes fractional precision from custom settling. Touch handoff keeps native dragging and starts a custom settle at release.

The fixture uses static colors, an 8 px gap, 28 px corners, and a 300 ms MD3 standard easing curve. The artwork is inline SVG. There are no external fonts, frameworks, analytics, or required accounts.
