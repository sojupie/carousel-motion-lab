Stepping as motion slows

Near the end of a release, card edges can hold across several frames and then advance in small steps. Earlier versions showed similar roughness on desktop and touch devices.

Regular clips play at ¼ speed; the handoff jump plays at ⅛ speed. Hold timings accumulate on the right and stay visible during the final four-second freeze. Coordinates and timestamps refer to the original recording.

Device results

Earlier versions stuttered on PC. Later desktop tests showed smoother motion. Touch stutter remained on iPhone 15, iPhone 13 Pro Max, and iPad Air (5th generation). Tests included Safari, Chrome, Firefox, and Brave on iOS. The later MacBook Air M4 test showed no stutter.

Native scrolling and Touch handoff felt smoother in initial phone tests. Further handoff tests exposed the overscroll regression. Desktop mouse release and baseline touch release use different settling paths.

Rendering and timing

Masked versions update a sticky visual track from logical scroll positions. The Native control uses normal flow. Rounded clock quantizes custom settling to whole pixels. Touch handoff starts custom settling at release.

The fixture uses inline SVG artwork, static colors, an 8 px gap, and 28 px corners. Custom settling uses Material Design 3 (MD3) standard easing over 300 ms.
